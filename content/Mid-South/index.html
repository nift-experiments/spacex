New supercomputers recycle water—cutting freshwater use by up to 70%. The system is filled once, and the same water is reused for up to 10 years.

What Colossus uses today

Colossus I has a hybrid system that uses about 820,000 gallons a day, similar to other industrial users in the area, while Colossus II uses closed-loop cooling and takes only domestic water. Nationwide, traditional data centers and supercomputers together use well under 1% of U.S. freshwater withdrawals.

Residential rates have not gone up

Traditional data centers in Memphis and Shelby County, and SpaceXAI’s supercomputer, have “unequivocally” not increased residential, commercial, or industrial rates, according to MLGW. Across the U.S., large computing loads help reduce average household power bills by paying a larger share of fixed grid costs and upgrades that would otherwise fall on homeowners.

SpaceXAI is investing in a stronger electric grid

In Memphis, SpaceXAI is investing $35 million in a 150 MW substation to support MLGW and another $20 million in a second substation. Both come online at no cost to MLGW or homeowners. TVA bills large computing loads under their own rate class, about 10% higher than the old manufacturing class, so operators rather than households bear the cost of new generation.

Installing resilient, sustainable backup power

SpaceXAI has spent hundreds of millions of dollars installing more than 240 batteries so Colossus I can come completely offline during emergencies or peak demand. By contract with TVA, large loads must cut power at peak so ratepayers are protected.

Filtration and emissions controls

SpaceXAI runs its turbines with modern, effective emissions-reduction equipment, including filtration systems. Local air continues to meet the EPA’s air quality standards.

Nearby monitors remain well under the federal standard

After temporary turbines started on August 1, 2025, expert analysis found no material worsening of NO₂, fine particle pollution, or ozone at nearby monitors. Annual NO₂ there is about 6–9 parts per billion. The federal standard is 53 ppb.

Temporary power is coming off

All remaining temporary units run in Tennessee and Mississippi with state authorization. Under an agreed order with the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality, all remaining temporary turbines must be removed by July 2027. SpaceXAI is already taking units offline and expects to complete removal well ahead of that deadline.
